# Research Principles

These principles govern how the project is researched, documented, and continued.

## 1. Do not repeat completed work

Do not repeat or restart material already covered. Continue from the latest documented point. Revisit an earlier finding only when new evidence, a material change, a contradiction, or a necessary validation justifies it.

## 2. Distinguish evidence from assumptions

Label provider claims, independently verified facts, project hypotheses, estimates, and unknowns. A website claim is not independent verification. A listed organisation is not a confirmed partner.

## 3. Verify before recommending

Do not describe an opportunity as viable until material requirements and commercial terms have been checked. Where provider confirmation is needed, record the exact question and the missing response.

## 4. Preserve the zero-capital constraint

Prioritise routes without upfront fees, compulsory purchases, inventory, equipment, paid licences, or mandatory paid certification. Identify hidden costs and defer opportunities that fail this filter unless the user explicitly changes the constraint.

## 5. Seek leverage through existing networks

Investigate organisations and intermediaries that already possess trust, customer access, professional authority, and distribution capacity. Do not equate follower counts or nominal membership with useful reach.

## 6. Require a value exchange

A partnership proposal must explain why the provider, intermediary, and end customer would participate. Do not approach a network merely to ask it to share a referral link.

## 7. Measure activation, not just reach

Distinguish nominal membership, active membership, communication access, decision-maker access, introductions, demonstrations, qualified leads, and actual adoption.

## 8. Keep business tracks separate

Do not merge different products, buyer groups, use cases, or partnership terms under one generic company label. For Farmonaut, agriculture and mining remain separate assessment tracks.

## 9. Record negative findings

Keep rejected and deferred opportunities, with reasons. This prevents cycling back to unsuitable options without new evidence.

## 10. Maintain traceability

Record source URLs, source types, access dates, evidence excerpts or summaries, confidence, verification status, and what changed. Link structured spreadsheet entries to detailed repository records.

## 11. No premature execution

The initial phase is research and planning. Do not assume that the user has approved outreach, paid commitments, implementation, or software development.

## 12. Update both records

After meaningful research sessions, update the repository's detailed records and the companion spreadsheet's structured records. Verify that both reflect the same status and decision. If direct Google Sheets access is unavailable, maintain an importable workbook and state that it requires user import.

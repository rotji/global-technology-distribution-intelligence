# Distribution Network Strategy

## Core idea

Instead of relying only on direct-to-customer affiliate promotion, identify existing organisations and intermediaries that already have relationships with the relevant buyer group. Establish an approved, mutually beneficial partnership that can make the technology discoverable, understandable, and adoptable through that network.

## Why this matters

A provider may have a useful product but limited market access. An association, cooperative, distributor, consultant, or sector institution may already communicate with the intended users. The project can create value by connecting the provider's solution with a relevant route to market and coordinating a credible value exchange.

## Network-owner categories

1. **Associations and federations:** professional bodies, sector associations, agricultural associations, mining associations, manufacturers' associations, and umbrella organisations.
2. **Distributors and aggregators:** agricultural input distributors, pharmaceutical wholesalers, equipment distributors, commodity aggregators, and supply-chain intermediaries.
3. **Industry connectors:** consultants, former executives, respected entrepreneurs, and sector specialists.
4. **Incubators and business-support organisations:** accelerators, enterprise-support programmes, and industry development organisations.
5. **Marketing and audience networks:** agencies, micro-influencer networks, specialist media, and community publishers.
6. **Professional service firms:** accountants, engineers, lawyers, IT consultants, agronomists, environmental consultants, and other trusted advisers.
7. **Financial and business networks:** banks, insurers, lenders, payment providers, and SME finance networks.
8. **Knowledge and community networks:** professional communities, online groups, newsletters, training organisations, and specialist media.

## Four dimensions of influence

- **Institutional:** authority within member organisations and formal structures.
- **Commercial:** access to buyers, suppliers, and purchasing relationships.
- **Professional:** technical credibility and trusted advice.
- **Audience:** ability to communicate with an identifiable audience.

The strongest intermediary may combine multiple dimensions.

## Network mapping questions

- What is the organisation's legal or operational identity?
- Who leads it, and who can authorise a partnership?
- Who are its members, customers, or beneficiaries?
- How many are active and relevant to the use case?
- Which geographies and subgroups does it reach?
- How does it communicate and convene members?
- Can it introduce, endorse, host a demonstration, or support adoption?
- Does it already work with competing or complementary providers?
- What would make participation worthwhile?
- What evidence supports its claimed reach and influence?

## Proposed partner scorecard (100 points)

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Network reach | 20 |
| Trust and credibility | 20 |
| Customer relevance | 20 |
| Activation ability | 15 |
| Commercial incentive | 10 |
| Access to decision-makers | 10 |
| Operational simplicity | 5 |
| **Total** | **100** |

Indicative internal interpretation: 80–100 strategic; 65–79 strong; 50–64 worth developing; below 50 low priority. These are project heuristics, not externally validated benchmarks. Scores should be accompanied by evidence and confidence.

## Distribution routes

- Institutional introduction to members.
- Chapter, branch, or cooperative-level engagement.
- Member education and workshops.
- Webinars or practical demonstrations.
- Newsletter, website, or approved messaging channels.
- Distributor or consultant referral.
- Co-branded programme or member benefit.
- Direct introduction to qualified buyers.

## Value exchange

Potential value to the intermediary includes a permitted referral fee, shared revenue, member discounts, useful technology services, sponsored education, or practical benefits for members. The provider must approve the structure, and any compensation must be transparent and compliant with applicable rules.

## Guardrails

- Do not imply endorsement without permission.
- Do not use member lists or private contact data without authority.
- Do not promise commissions or benefits before provider confirmation.
- Do not mistake a large nominal network for active reach.
- Do not create multi-level recruitment incentives. Compensation should relate to genuine product referrals, sales, or services, not recruitment of sub-affiliates unless specifically lawful and provider-approved.

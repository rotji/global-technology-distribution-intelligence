# Data Marketplaces and Intermediaries

**Research status:** Preliminary comparative notes  
**As-of date:** 21 November 2026

## Purpose
Understand how platforms connect data supply and demand. Distinguish a marketplace from a data buyer, broker, sourcing partner, supplier programme or data-preparation provider.

## Platforms and models examined

### DataFactor
Official website: https://datafactor.com/

- Describes connecting AI companies with owners of proprietary data.
- Published model includes sourcing, evaluation, preparation, anonymisation/normalisation and licensing/delivery.
- Published material emphasises direct licensing from data owners and rights/consent verification.
- Case-study deal values are company-published claims and have not been independently audited in this research.
- No public standard independent Scout/referral commission programme was established in the reviewed material.
- Potential route: direct data owner, negotiated sourcing partner or broker relationship. Terms and individual eligibility require direct confirmation.

### Defined.ai
Official partnership information: https://defined.ai/partnership-programs  
Supplier information: https://defined.ai/supplier-program

- Public routes include data partners and AI service suppliers.
- These routes appear oriented toward organisations able to supply data assets or deliver collection/annotation services, rather than a simple individual referral commission.
- Published supplier conditions include company-authority, compliance, data ethics and rights requirements. Reviewed supplier terms also describe a licence for samples to be shared with customers to gauge interest and include indemnity and non-circumvention provisions.
- No standard referral commission or supplier payout schedule was established in reviewed public material.
- Treat as a possible future supplier/service route, not a confirmed no-capital individual opportunity.

### Data Partners HQ
Official website: https://datapartnershq.com/

- Presents itself as an intermediary connecting company data owners with buyers and referral sources.
- Promotional material describes referral fees and deal sizes, but these are not independently verified or guaranteed.
- Its published qualification profile focuses on established companies and selected geographies. Nigeria was not included in the country list reviewed for its closed-company programme.
- Useful as an intermediary-model example, not a confirmed Nigerian data-owner route.

## Distinctions to verify
| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Does the platform buy data itself? | Buyer obligations differ from marketplace/broker obligations. |
| Does the owner license directly to the end buyer? | Contracting and payment paths differ. |
| Does the platform own or facilitate the relationship? | Determines control of the transaction and rights. |
| Can an individual source data owners? | Supplier eligibility may be company-only. |
| Can a Nigerian participant join and receive payment? | Global marketing does not establish local eligibility. |
| Are referral fees contractual? | Promotional figures may be conditional or discretionary. |
| Are samples required? | Sample rights, privacy and confidentiality need review. |

## Conclusion
These examples show multiple distinct service layers in AI data supply. They do not establish that a Nigerian individual can immediately earn by uploading or reselling data. Obtain clear written terms before pursuing a supplier or sourcing relationship.

# Network Intelligence Methodology

## Purpose

Identify, profile, and rank intermediaries that may provide legitimate access to a technology provider's intended customer group.

## Network record

Capture:
- Unique network ID and organisation name.
- Type and sector.
- Official website and public contact channels.
- Legal/operational identity and legitimacy evidence.
- Geography and relevant local branches.
- Stated membership or reach, with source and date.
- Evidence of active membership and recent activity.
- Leadership and partnership decision-makers.
- Communication and convening channels.
- Relevant member/customer segments.
- Existing commercial or technology relationships.
- Ability to introduce, convene, educate, endorse, refer, or distribute.
- Incentives and potential conflicts.
- Evidence confidence and unresolved questions.

## Network activation ladder

Distinguish the following stages:
1. Organisation identified.
2. Identity and activity checked.
3. Relevant decision-maker identified.
4. Contact route verified.
5. Introduction obtained.
6. Interest expressed.
7. Partnership authority and terms discussed.
8. Distribution mechanism agreed.
9. Members or customers reached.
10. Qualified leads or demonstrations generated.
11. Adoption or sales evidenced.

Do not describe an organisation as a distribution partner merely because it has been identified or contacted.

## Scorecard

Use the project's weighted scorecard in the distribution strategy document. Score only with evidence; mark unknown dimensions as unknown rather than assigning optimistic points.

## Relationship mapping

Map both direct routes and potential connectors. A connector's relevance depends on whether it can make a credible introduction to the specific decision-maker or buyer group, not just whether it is influential in general.

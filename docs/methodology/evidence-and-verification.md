# Evidence and Verification

## Purpose

Keep the repository's factual claims reliable, traceable and proportionate to the evidence. The project may document research principles, ecosystem architectures, topic taxonomies and questions without asserting that the listed entities or conditions exist. Factual research records must pass the documentation gate below.

## Evidence hierarchy

Use the following practical hierarchy, considering relevance and recency:
1. Current written confirmation from the provider or organisation's authorised representative.
2. Current official programme terms, policies, public registers, formal records or primary research documents.
3. Official organisational pages and dated announcements.
4. Credible independent reporting or sector sources.
5. Third-party directories and event listings.
6. Social posts, informal comments and uncorroborated claims.

The hierarchy is a guide, not an absolute rule; a source may be authoritative for one claim but not another. For example, an official announcement can establish that an organisation announced an initiative, but it does not by itself establish implementation, beneficiary participation, disbursement or current operating status.

## Documentation gate

Do not add unverified leads, rumours, copied search results, speculative relationships or unsupported opportunity claims to GitHub as factual findings.

Before documenting a factual claim:
- State the exact claim and define its geographic and temporal scope.
- Confirm that the source directly supports that exact claim.
- Check who issued the source, its original context, publication/update date and whether it is sufficiently current.
- Separate the source's own statement from independently corroborated reality.
- Seek independent or primary-source corroboration for consequential operational, legal, scientific, financial, commercial or relationship claims.
- Preserve contradictions and limitations rather than silently reconciling them.
- Do not infer local presence, reach, capacity, eligibility, demand, partnership, implementation or benefit from a broad mandate or general announcement.

If the evidence is insufficient, keep the item in the active research process or temporary working notes. Do not promote it to a factual repository record. Where useful for continuity, document the neutral research question without repeating the unverified proposition as fact.

Research architecture documents may list topics such as laboratories, policies, standards, information networks or funding mechanisms as areas to investigate. Such a list is a scope definition, not evidence that a particular facility, policy, programme or service exists locally.

## Required evidence fields

For each material factual claim, record:
- Exact claim.
- Geographic and temporal scope.
- Source title and URL.
- Source type and issuing organisation.
- Publication/update date, if available.
- Date accessed.
- Relevant excerpt or concise summary.
- What the source directly establishes.
- What it does not establish or what remains uncertain.
- Whether the claim is self-reported or independently corroborated.
- Confidence and verification status.
- Reason the finding matters and any justified next verification step.

## Status definitions

- **Source-supported:** a traceable source directly supports the limited claim as worded; this does not automatically prove broader operational reality.
- **Independently corroborated:** material parts of the claim are supported by suitable independent or primary evidence.
- **Provider-confirmed:** provider has explicitly confirmed the relevant point.
- **Network-confirmed:** the organisation or authorised representative has confirmed the relevant point.
- **Qualified:** key commercial, market and access requirements are evidenced.
- **Conditional:** plausible but material questions remain; do not present as established fact.
- **Deferred:** not actionable under present constraints.
- **Rejected:** fails a required criterion or evidence contradicts the proposition.

Do not use a status label to make a weak source appear stronger than it is. Explain the exact scope of confirmation.

## Source discipline

Do not fabricate citations or treat an inaccessible page as evidence. Retain older findings with their original dates and re-check them when they materially affect a decision. Record contradictions instead of silently choosing the more convenient claim.

## Special caution for institutional and programme claims

- An announced programme is not proof of implementation.
- A budget, allocation or commitment is not proof of disbursement or receipt.
- A beneficiary target is not a verified beneficiary register.
- A facility's existence or construction is not proof of operational capacity, service availability or affordable access.
- A stated institutional mandate is not proof of current activity or geographic coverage.
- A membership list is not proof of active members, representation or useful reach.
- A reported partnership is not proof of an agreed, active relationship.
- A law or policy proposal is not an enacted or enforced instrument.
- A published study's sample is not a census of the wider population.
- A provider's product description is not independent evidence of performance or local suitability.
- A production or harvest claim is not proof of repeatable yield, profitability or market demand.

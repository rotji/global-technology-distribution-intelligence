# Evidence and Verification

## Evidence hierarchy

Use the following practical hierarchy, considering relevance and recency:
1. Current written confirmation from the provider or organisation's authorised representative.
2. Current official programme terms, policies, public registers, or formal documents.
3. Official organisational pages and dated announcements.
4. Credible independent reporting or sector sources.
5. Third-party directories and event listings.
6. Social posts, informal comments, and uncorroborated claims.

The hierarchy is a guide, not an absolute rule; a source may be authoritative for one claim but not another.

## Required evidence fields

For each material claim, record:
- Claim.
- Source title and URL.
- Source type and issuing organisation.
- Publication/update date, if available.
- Date accessed.
- Relevant excerpt or concise summary.
- Whether the claim is self-reported or independently corroborated.
- Confidence: High, Medium, or Low.
- Verification status and next action.

## Status definitions

- **Unverified lead:** identified, but material facts not yet checked.
- **Evidence found:** a source supports the claim, but the claim may not be independently confirmed.
- **Provider-confirmed:** provider has explicitly confirmed the relevant point.
- **Network-confirmed:** the organisation or authorised representative has confirmed the relevant point.
- **Qualified:** key commercial, market, and access requirements are met.
- **Conditional:** plausible but material questions remain.
- **Deferred:** not actionable under present constraints.
- **Rejected:** fails a required criterion or evidence contradicts the proposition.

## Source discipline

Do not fabricate citations or treat an inaccessible page as evidence. Retain older findings with their original dates and re-check them when they materially affect a decision. Record contradictions instead of silently choosing the more convenient claim.

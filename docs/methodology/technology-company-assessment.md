# Technology Company Assessment Methodology

## Purpose

Assess whether a provider's product solves a real problem and whether a legitimate, commercially accessible partnership can connect it to a viable customer group.

## Company profile

Record:
- Company name and official website.
- Product(s) and product category.
- Main problems solved and evidence of capability.
- Target users, buyer, budget owner, and decision-maker.
- Geography and language support.
- Deployment and infrastructure requirements.
- Pricing model and purchasing route.
- Customer evidence, case studies, and independent validation.
- Risks, dependencies, and alternatives.

## Capability and solution fit

Assess the provider in relation to the capability required by the underlying opportunity.

Record:
- The specific capability or outcome the product provides.
- Whether the capability is essential, supplementary or optional.
- What the product can demonstrably accomplish.
- What it cannot accomplish or requires from other services.
- Whether the capability already exists within the project environment or through other accessible providers.
- Whether the provider's solution can work alongside existing platforms, services and resources.
- Whether integration, implementation, configuration or a partnership is required.
- Whether the product introduces additional technical, commercial or operational dependencies.

Do not assume that a provider is necessary simply because its product is relevant to the sector.

Assess whether the provider supplies a capability that is genuinely required and whether it is more suitable than reuse, combination or integration of existing alternatives.

## Partnership profile

Record:
- Programme name and official terms URL.
- Model: affiliate, referral, reseller, channel, institutional, or other.
- Eligibility and geographic restrictions.
- Individual/company requirements.
- Fees, purchases, inventory, certification, or sales targets.
- Compensation and qualifying event.
- Recurring or one-time nature.
- Tracking and attribution.
- Payout method, currency, minimum, and timing.
- Network distribution permissions.
- Support, responsibilities, and termination conditions.

## Market fit

Assess:
- Is the underlying problem present in the target market?
- Is the product suitable for local infrastructure, language, regulation, and purchasing power?
- Who experiences the problem most acutely?
- Who has budget and authority to buy?
- What proof would a customer need before purchase?
- Which existing network already serves that customer group?

## Dependency, proportionality and reassessment

Assess:
- Whether the proposed opportunity depends on a single provider.
- Whether credible substitutes or complementary providers exist.
- Whether the provider can be replaced without disproportionate disruption.
- Whether its pricing, access, eligibility and service conditions are sustainable.
- Whether the solution creates maintenance, training, support or operational obligations.
- Whether the provider's contribution justifies the additional complexity it introduces.

Record material dependencies and concentration risks.

Reassess the provider when significant changes occur in product capability, pricing, availability, eligibility, service quality, commercial terms or customer requirements.

Preserve earlier assessments and explain any change in recommendation.

## Decision

Before qualifying a provider, establish whether its capability is actually required and whether existing capabilities or a combination of alternatives can meet the need.

A provider may be technically suitable but commercially inaccessible, unnecessarily complex or redundant with an existing capability.

The decision should distinguish:
- Provider capability suitability.
- Customer and market suitability.
- Partnership accessibility.
- Commercial viability.
- Operational feasibility.
- Strategic dependency and alternatives.

Do not treat technical suitability alone as proof of a viable opportunity.

Use **Qualified**, **Conditional**, **Deferred**, or **Rejected**. State evidence, uncertainty, key risks, and the next validation needed. Do not assign a definitive score when core facts are unknown.
